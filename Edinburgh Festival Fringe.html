Share your views on the Fringe's economic, social and wellbeing impact

26 October 2026 News
  • Home

  • Share your views on the Fringe's economic, social and wellbeing impact

The survey forms part of an independent study being conducted by Glasgow University researchers.

Fringe audiences are being invited to share their thoughts on the festival as part of an independent assessment of its economic and social impact conducted by researchers from the University of Glasgow, led by Professor Murray Pittock.

The questionnaire examines core economic measures, including attendance, ticket spending, overnight stays and the impact of visitor spending on food, drink and other local attractions. 

In addition, the survey explores whether attending the Fringe affects people's wellbeing, including their sense of happiness, belonging, social connectedness and overall quality of life. The wellbeing section also explores whether attending the Fringe feels worthwhile and how it contributes to experiences such as creative inspiration, social connection, exposure to new ideas and trying new things. 

The study will also explore the role Edinburgh plays in the Fringe experience, examining how the city's character, cultural reputation, hospitality offer and atmosphere contribute to the festival’s appeal as a globally significant cultural event.

Professor Murray Pittock, Pro-Vice-Principal at the University of Glasgow, said: 'When we think about the value of the Fringe, it is about much more than ticket sales and visitor spending. The festival brings people together, exposes audiences to new experiences and ideas, and can have a lasting impact on wellbeing. 

'This survey will help us understand not only the Fringe’s economic contribution, but also the social and cultural benefits it delivers to those who attend.'

Tony Lankester, Chief Executive of the Edinburgh Festival Fringe Society, said: 'Audiences from Scotland and around the world are central to the success of the Edinburgh Fringe, and their experiences can help the Fringe Society to better understand the impact the Fringe has as a global expression of culture in Scotland. I encourage anyone with a stake in the Fringe to complete the University of Glasgow questionnaire. It is a vital opportunity to ensure the Fringe audience's views are represented and to help shape the findings.  

'Professor Pittock’s research is vital to demonstrating the value of the Edinburgh Fringe as the most significant platform for emerging talent to express their stories. These findings will help the Fringe Society to further understand the Edinburgh Fringe’s economic, social and cultural benefits to Scotland's cultural economy.'

Professor Pittock was commissioned by UKRI World Class Laboratories in summer 2026 to undertake a year-long analysis investigation into how the Edinburgh Festival Fringe supports jobs and businesses and contributes to Scotland's wider cultural economy. 

Professor Christopher Smith, Executive Chair of the UKRI’s Arts and Humanities Research Council (AHRC), said: 'We at UKRI have been increasingly investing in establishing the value of culture, not least through the Culture and Heritage Capital programme. 

'This survey uses as its laboratory one of the greatest single cultural festivals in a city full of heritage and with a global reach. I look forward to seeing how the results will deepen our understanding and enrich our policy making in Scotland and beyond.'
