A Water Lily Sign in a Woman's Pancreas Exposed a Tapeworm Cyst That Had Already Been Needled

A 34-year-old woman in Iran had a cyst in the head of her pancreas, and doctors at another hospital passed a needle into it to sample the fluid. Four days later, an MRI showed a crumpled membrane drifting inside the cyst like a water lily on a pond. That image pointed to a cyst formed by a parasitic tapeworm, the kind that specialists say should not be routinely punctured once imaging suggests it.

The case, published Oct. 13 in BJR|Case Reports, comes from radiologists at Imam Khomeini Hospital and Tehran University of Medical Sciences. They describe a pancreatic hydatid cyst, an uncommon location for an infection that usually settles in the liver or lungs.

Belly Pain and a Cyst That Looked Like a Tumor

The woman had intermittent pain in her upper abdomen that spread to her back, along with occasional nausea. Her exam showed only mild tenderness. Routine blood tests, including liver enzymes and inflammation markers, were normal, and the tumor markers CEA and CA 19-9 were not elevated.

An ultrasound found a well-defined cyst in the head of the pancreas. Before she was referred to the authors' center, an outside team performed endoscopic ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration, drawing fluid through a needle passed from the digestive tract. The cyst looked simple on that exam.

The fluid helped narrow things down. Its amylase level was just 20 U/L, which argued against a pseudocyst, the fluid collection that often follows pancreatitis. CEA was undetectable, making a mucinous cyst, a type that can become cancerous, less likely.

The Membrane That Changed the Diagnosis

The MRI, which included a specialized scan of the bile and pancreatic ducts, showed a thin-walled cyst about 3 to 4 centimeters across. Inside floated a wavy, detached membrane. The main pancreatic duct upstream was mildly widened.

That mattered because the list of possibilities for a single large cyst in a young woman's pancreas includes several tumors, among them mucinous cystic neoplasms, intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms, serous cystadenomas, solid pseudopapillary neoplasms and cystic neuroendocrine tumors. Some can become cancerous, and each calls for a different plan.

Radiologists call the floating membrane the water lily sign. It forms when the inner layer of a hydatid cyst separates from the wall and floats in the fluid. The authors describe it as rare but highly characteristic of a cyst in a transitional stage of the disease.

The membrane had not been described on the earlier endoscopic ultrasound. The authors could not tell whether it detached on its own, was missed the first time or resulted from the needle procedure.

A blood test for antibodies to the parasite came back negative. That did not rule out the infection. A 2021 review of 33 primary pancreatic hydatid cysts reported between 2011 and 2021 found that 14 sat in the head of the pancreas, as hers did, and that 8 of the 19 patients with reported antibody results tested negative.

A Parasite Spread by Dogs

Cystic echinococcosis is caused by larvae of the tapeworm Echinococcus granulosus. Dogs that eat organs from infected sheep and other livestock carry the adult worms and pass eggs in their stool, and people become infected by swallowing those eggs, according to the CDC. The cysts grow slowly and may cause no symptoms for years.

The liver and lungs account for most cases. Citing a systematic review of pancreatic hydatid surgery, the Tehran authors note that these cysts can imitate pancreatic tumors and pseudocysts.

The puncture matters because hydatid cysts contain fluid and parasite material that can trigger severe allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, and can spread the infection if spilled. No immediate complication was recorded after her procedure, which the authors called fortunate.

Surgery, Six Months of Medicine and No Recurrence

Thirteen days after she first came in, a multidisciplinary team concluded the cyst was most consistent with a stage CE3a hydatid cyst under World Health Organization staging. Because of the duct widening and lingering concern about a tumor, they recommended surgery.

She started the antiparasitic drug albendazole around the time of the operation. On day 25, surgeons removed the cyst whole and deliberately avoided flushing parasite-killing solutions into the bile ducts, which can cause chemical scarring. Pathologists found the cyst's layered membranes and protoscolices, the larval heads of the tapeworm, confirming the diagnosis.

She completed six months of albendazole with monthly liver tests and no side effects. MRI scans at six and 12 months showed no recurrence, and she has returned to her usual activities. She told her doctors that having a clear diagnosis and a plan that avoided repeat needle procedures eased her anxiety.

The authors' takeaway for radiologists is direct: where the parasite is common, a pancreatic cyst with a floating membrane should raise suspicion of hydatid disease before anyone reaches for a needle.

The infection is rare in the United States, where most cases are diagnosed in immigrants from countries where it is common, according to the CDC. Anyone with persistent, unexplained abdominal pain should discuss it with a clinician.

Key Questions Answered

What is the water lily sign?

An imaging finding in which a detached inner membrane floats inside a hydatid cyst, resembling a water lily on a pond.

What causes hydatid cysts?

Larvae of the tapeworm Echinococcus granulosus, usually after people swallow eggs passed in infected dogs' stool.

Why is puncturing a suspected hydatid cyst risky?

Spilled contents can cause severe allergic reactions and spread the infection.

Did a blood test confirm the infection?

No. Her antibody test was negative, which happens in a sizable share of pancreatic cases. Surgery and pathology confirmed it.

How was she treated?

Surgical removal of the cyst plus six months of albendazole. Follow-up MRIs at six and 12 months were clear.

Is this infection common in the U.S.?

No. It is rare, and most U.S. cases are diagnosed in immigrants from countries where it is common.
