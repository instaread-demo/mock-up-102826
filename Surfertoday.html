Tristan Roberts crowned 2026 bodyboarding world champion

October 27, 2026  |  Bodyboarding

South Africa's Tristan Roberts is a three-time world champion after mathematically securing the 2026 men's world championship at the Gran Canaria Frontón King.

Roberts arrived in Gran Canaria leading the world tour rankings and needed only to compete in his opening heat at El Frontón to mathematically secure the title.

But the South African did much more than simply enter the water: he won his Round 4 heat with a 13.25 total, ahead of Peru's Christopher Bayona on 12.25, before returning to shore as a three-time men's world champion.

What followed was one of the most emotional moments of this year's Frontón King.

Two of Roberts' childhood friends carried him out of the water on their shoulders, while his mother was waiting for him in the riders' area.

She had traveled to Gran Canaria as a surprise to witness her son win a world title in person for the first time.

"It's super special for me to become world champion here again," Roberts said after leaving the water.

A special relationship with El Frontón

There are few places as closely connected to Roberts' career as El Frontón.

The South African first discovered the wave when he was just 12 years old and has returned to Gran Canaria many times since, spending extended periods training at El Frontón, getting to know the local riders and becoming part of the community surrounding one of the world's most renowned bodyboarding waves.

Here, Roberts secured his first men's world championship in 2019, the same year he won the Frontón King.

He won his second world crown at El Frontón in 2022, and four years later, Gran Canaria has once again provided the setting for another defining moment in his career.

The 2026 title is Roberts' third men's world championship, following his 2019 and 2022 victories. He was also crowned pro junior world champion in 2014.

But the 29-year-old says his perspective has changed considerably since winning his first senior crown seven years ago.

He explained that the hunger of a young rider desperate to become world champion drove that first title.

Ground support

Today, after experiencing how difficult it is to win and stay at the top, he approaches every heat, every round, every event, and every title with much greater gratitude.

That feeling was especially strong on Monday because he could share the moment with the people he loves.

Roberts described his mother as his "number one fan" and one of the main reasons he has reached the position he occupies today.

She supported and encouraged him throughout his childhood and helped him pursue a career in bodyboarding.

Having her alongside his partner and childhood friends in Gran Canaria made his third world title an especially personal celebration.
